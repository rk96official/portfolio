INSIGHT-HPC: An Interpretable System Intelligence for Global HPC Telemetry

Rupak Kadel, Jie Li, Yong Chen · IEEE BigData '26

Motivation

Modern HPC clusters mix generations of CPUs and GPUs that stream rich telemetry from baseboard management controllers (BMCs), job schedulers, and facility power distribution units (PDUs). Most detectors look at one telemetry plane at a time, rely on statistical deviation alone, and need stronger labels than production sites can provide. They also give operators little context: they often cannot tell a heavy job from a failing node.

INSIGHT-HPC progressively refines statistical deviations using workload context and physical consistency, then fuses the evidence into de-duplicated fault episodes with explanations operators can act on.

Architecture

Sources

  • BMC sensors (Redfish)
  • Slurm jobs
  • Rack PDUs
→

Preprocess

  • Quality auditing
  • Per-minute master table
  • Regime-aware features
→

Detect

I Statistical: rules, Isolation Forest, LSTM-AE
II Job-aware: segmentation, archetypes, peers
III Physics: power/thermal residuals, constraints
→

IV · Fuse

  • Episode formation
  • Gradient-boosted classifier
  • TreeSHAP attribution
→

Alerts

  • CRITICAL
  • CONFIRMED
  • CANDIDATE
  • Phase I, Statistical detectors. Threshold rules, Isolation Forest, and an LSTM autoencoder screen each node-minute against its own history. Scores are converted to per-host trailing ranks, so firing rates stay stable when operating regimes shift.
  • Phase II, Job-aware modeling. Node timelines are segmented at Slurm job boundaries, and segments are clustered into workload archetypes. Nodes are scored against similar workloads and against their peers in the same job (Hampel z-score). On shared nodes, anomalies are attributed to the most likely job.
  • Phase III, Physics reasoning. Per-node-type Ridge models predict expected power and inlet temperature. Residuals are scaled with a rolling 14-day IQR. Four constraint rules check dynamics, cross-plane (PDU vs. BMC) agreement, allocation vs. utilization, and cooling response.
  • Phase IV, Fusion. Per-minute evidence is grouped into episodes, and a histogram gradient-boosting classifier assigns each one to an operating tier. Every alert ships with job context, violated constraints, dominant signals, and model attribution.

Evaluation

The evaluation uses 79 days (Jan 1 – Mar 20, 2026) of production telemetry from the 118-node compute fleet of the NSF-funded REPACSS cluster: ~220K node-hours and 13,266 Slurm jobs. The test set has 169 labeled events across 85 hosts: 29 operator-recorded failures and 140 controlled fault injections covering thermal, power, cooling, resource, sensing, and cross-plane failure modes.

Reproducing FCG-NO: Neural Operator Preconditioners for the Flexible Conjugate Gradient Method

Batuhan Sencer, Rupak Kadel, Abhijeet Pandey · Texas Tech University · 2026

Background

Neural operators can solve PDEs in a single forward pass, but their accuracy usually stalls between 0.1% and 1% relative error. FCG-NO (Rudikov et al., ICML '24) gets around this limit by using the network as a preconditioner inside Notay's flexible conjugate gradient (FCG) method, which tolerates nonlinear preconditioners. The solver keeps classical accuracy and uses the network only to converge faster. The operator is trained with a loss derived from Notay's convergence theorem, on residuals collected from CG runs.

What We Built

We rebuilt the full pipeline from scratch as a new JAX package, consulting the authors' repository only as a reference. It includes:

  • Data generation, Krylov-residual collection, and batched GPU implementations of CG and FCG
  • Four preconditioner networks (spectral neural operator, FNO, dilated ResNet, U-Net) with the Notay and L2 losses
  • Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, and ILU baselines, plus three CG timing implementations
  • 93 automated tests. Loaded with the authors' weights, our networks match their outputs to floating-point precision, and FCG with the identity preconditioner reproduces CG iteration by iteration.

For every experiment, we compared four sources where they exist: the published results, the authors' saved notebook runs, the authors' unmodified code, and our reimplementation.

  • Reproduced: classical baselines (within four iterations once the matrix scaling was corrected), the advantage of the Notay loss over L2, the advantage of Krylov residuals over random ones for training, and a per-step Notay ratio near 0.5.
  • Not reproduced: the headline iteration counts. The authors' unmodified code produces our numbers, not the published ones. At 128×128, the solver stalls on part of the test set.
  • Timing reverses: against a JIT-compiled CG baseline, each FCG-SNO iteration costs roughly 36× more (up to ~200× at the largest grid). FCG-SNO ends up slower overall despite needing fewer iterations.

Diagnosis & Proposed Fixes

On stalled systems, the per-step Notay ratio crosses 1 between iterations 7 and 24. Past that point, the theorem no longer guarantees contraction. We identify two gaps between the theory and the training procedure:

  1. The guarantee needs the preconditioner error below 1 at every step, but the loss controls only its average.
  2. The network is trained on CG residuals but applied to FCG residuals.

We propose training on the solver's own residuals with a worst-case (CVaR) loss, a safety-net FCG variant that always keeps the plain residual direction, a coefficient-aware network input, reduced-precision inference, and a smoothing wrapper. Each proposal is paired with an experiment that could falsify it.

Accurate Calculation of the Earth's Pressure Using Numerical Integration

LaGuardia Community College · December 2017

Abstract

This study calculated the pressure inside the Earth as a function of distance from the center using two Earth models. Comparing them shows why realistic density profiles matter in geophysical calculations.

Key Findings

  • A center pressure of about 364 GPa under the Preliminary Reference Earth Model (PREM)
  • A uniform-density model underestimates the center pressure by a factor of 2 (172 GPa vs. 364 GPa)
  • A rotational inertia of 8.02×10³⁷ kg·m², 20% lower than the uniform-density estimate
  • Mass concentrated near the Earth's center significantly changes its rotational properties

Methodology

  • Pressure equations derived from hydrostatic equilibrium in spherical coordinates
  • Simpson's 1/3 rule applied to the PREM density profile
  • Enclosed mass computed as a function of radius for accurate gravitational force
  • Implemented in Python with SciPy
